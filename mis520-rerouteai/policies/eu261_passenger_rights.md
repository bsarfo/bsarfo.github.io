# EU Regulation 261/2004 - passenger rights (summary for the prototype)

Source: Regulation (EC) No 261/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council; Court of Justice of the EU case law. This is a simplified summary for a class prototype, not legal advice. The team must verify every statement against the official text (eur-lex.europa.eu) before the final presentation.

## Scope - which journeys are covered
The regulation covers flights departing from an airport in the EU, and flights arriving in the EU from a non-EU airport when the operating airline is an EU carrier. Switzerland, Norway and Iceland apply the regulation through agreements with the EU, so a Swiss carrier arriving in Zürich from Boston is treated like an EU carrier. A US carrier flying from Boston to Europe is not covered on that leg.

## Long delay at the final destination
The CJEU (Sturgeon, C-402/07 and C-432/07) held that passengers who reach their final destination three hours or more after the scheduled arrival are entitled to compensation, as for a cancellation. For a journey with connecting flights booked on one reservation, the delay is measured at the final destination (Folkerts, C-11/11), not at the connecting airport.

## Compensation amounts
Compensation depends on the great-circle distance of the whole journey: EUR 250 for journeys of 1,500 km or less; EUR 400 for intra-EU journeys over 1,500 km and other journeys of 1,500 to 3,500 km; EUR 600 for journeys over 3,500 km. When the passenger is re-routed and arrives within 2, 3 or 4 hours of the original arrival time (for the three distance bands respectively), the airline may reduce compensation by 50 percent. Boston to Warsaw is over 3,500 km, so a delay of 3 to 4 hours can cost EUR 300 and a delay of 4 hours or more EUR 600.

## Extraordinary circumstances
No compensation is owed when the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken, such as severe weather, air traffic control restrictions, security risks or political instability. Most technical faults and crew shortages are not extraordinary circumstances. Right to care still applies.

## Right to care
While waiting for a re-routed flight, passengers are entitled to meals and refreshments in reasonable relation to the waiting time, two telephone calls or emails, and hotel accommodation plus transport between the airport and the hotel when an overnight stay becomes necessary.

## Re-routing and refund
When a flight is cancelled or a connection is missed on a single booking, the passenger can choose between a refund, re-routing to the final destination at the earliest opportunity under comparable transport conditions, or re-routing at a later date of their choosing. Re-routing at the earliest opportunity can include flights on other airlines when the carrier's own flights would arrive significantly later.
